Supplies

Basic Info

Getting started with garage kits requires a variety of tools and materials. Some supplies are essential, while others simply make the process easier. You may even have a few of these items lying around already!

Before getting started, it is important to prioritize your safety. Resin dust can be harmful when inhaled, so always wear a properly fitted N95 or better respirator when sanding or drilling resin. When working with lacquer paints, use a properly fitted respirator with cartridges rated for organic vapors and particulate matter. Work in a well-ventilated area and follow the safety instructions for your materials.

Prep & Assembly Supplies

Before painting, garage kit pieces need to be cleaned, sanded, and prepared. Some of the most useful tools for this process include wire cutters, sandpaper, a hobby or X-Acto knife, a pin vise or hand drill, drill bits, metal wires or pins, putty, and resin wash.

Sandpaper comes in different grits and is used to smooth rough surfaces, remove imperfections, and clean up leftover resin from the molding process. A hobby knife and wire cutters help remove excess resin and clean up small details.

A hand drill and metal pins are useful for pinning pieces together. This helps strengthen connections between larger or heavier parts. Putty can be used to fill gaps between pieces and fix small imperfections.

For assembly, super glue is one of the most commonly used adhesives. It creates a strong bond between resin pieces and is especially useful for attaching smaller parts.

Paint Supplies

Painting is one of the most exciting parts of building a garage kit! There are several different ways to paint your figure, but an airbrush is one of the most popular tools because it allows for smooth, even coats and detailed color gradients.

For this tutorial, we will be using an airbrush. You'll also need an air compressor, paints, paint thinner, and cleaning supplies.

Other useful painting supplies include primer, masking tape, paint brushes, mixing cups, paint holders, and alligator clips. Primer helps paint adhere to the resin surface, while masking tape allows you to protect certain areas when painting multiple colors.

You do not necessarily need an airbrush to get started! Paint brushes can be used for smaller details and touch-ups. However, if you plan on building more garage kits in the future, investing in an airbrush setup can make the painting process much easier.

A Few Extra Tips

You do not need to purchase every tool at once. Start with the essentials and gradually build up your collection as you become more experienced. Some tools are more useful depending on the size, complexity, and number of pieces in your kit.

Remember to keep your workspace organized and clean your tools after each use. Taking care of your supplies will help them last longer and make future projects easier!
